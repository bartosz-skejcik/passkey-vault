## Task 3 -- E-S4: does a backgrounded app on this simulator actually lose its socket?

`subsystem == "cloud.blonie.PasskeyVault" and category == "sync"` for the whole run. Socket open confirmed
(`PVSYNC|event=open`), one push observed and rendered (`PVSYNC|event=frame` + matching `render`) BEFORE

**Backgrounding:** no `xcrun simctl` subcommand sends an app to the background directly, so this run used the
equivalent a Home-button press produces at the OS level -- launching a DIFFERENT app

Twelve `PVSYNC|event=frame` lines -- one per background mutation -- appear in a tight burst, all timestamped at
or immediately after the moment the app was foregrounded again, not spread across the 193s background window.
No `PVSYNC|event=close` line appears anywhere in the log for this run's connection. Only the final render fired
(the receive loop drained all 12 queued frames and issued 12 pulls, but by the time the LAST one's pull
completed the item was already at its final, revision-13 state, so the app's own probe only ever OBSERVED the
last value -- consistent with 12 frames each triggering a pull, not with a stuck or one-shot receive).

**Reading this result:** the socket was never closed and no frame was lost -- all 12 mutations reached the
still-open connection and were delivered/processed once the process resumed being scheduled. This is
**Result B**: the Simulator does **not** reproduce device suspension. On this simulator, a backgrounded process
appears to have its CPU scheduling suspended (nothing is logged during the 193s window) while its network
socket is left alive by the host, rather than the OS tearing the connection down as it would on a real device
under memory/power pressure. SC4's copy requirement still stands (it is a statement about devices, not about
this simulator), but the **proof** that a socket eventually goes stale must come from 39-06's server-stop
fallback path, not from a Simulator backgrounding test -- this run demonstrates why: on this Simulator, sockets
do not visibly die from backgrounding, so a passing backgrounding test here would not distinguish "correctly
observing device behaviour" from "the Simulator simply keeps everything alive." No sentence in this file claims
backgrounding was tested as a proof mechanism for a stale-socket close; it was tested, and it did not close.

**Standing limitation:** even under Result A this timing would be simulator- and host-power-state-dependent and
would not transfer to a real device; under the Result B observed here, the non-closure itself is simulator-only
and must not be read as evidence that a real, backgrounded iOS device keeps a WebSocket alive indefinitely --
the opposite is true on real hardware, and SC4's copy is written for that reality, not this one.
